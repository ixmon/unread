About Unread Atlas

Why this exists

These are humanity's oldest known writings: receipts, prayers, letters, lullabies and science. For generations, reading them took a PhD, a trip to a faraway land and luck at the museum, and most of us only saw them through someone else's filter. Unread Atlas gathers what is openly published and lays it out so everyone can read it unfiltered.

What's here

462,298 cuneiform tablets catalogued from five open projects (CDLI, ORACC, eBL, TLHdig and Achemenet), each counted once. 223,678 have a transliteration online, 21,451 have English by human scholars and 165,344 more have a machine translation. 316,227 are on the map. Every figure, by source, period and language, is on the Stats page.

The machine English

Where no human translation is online, the English column shows a cached translation from B2c (T5-large), montycrypto/atf-to-english, which reads ATF one line at a time. It is labelled “Machine translation” everywhere it appears, and the reader can show it side by side with human English wherever both exist.

Ways in

  • Map & time: find-spots and a period scrubber
  • Reader: English, ATF and period-style cuneiform side by side
  • Search, Browse and Signs across the whole corpus
  • Featured: hand-picked sets of epic, remedy, omen and math tablets

Cuneiform is drawn in a period-matched face, following the eBL converter; the reader's “Cuneiform face” menu lets you pick another (font credits).

Credits & data. The catalogue and texts come from CDLI, ORACC, eBL, TLHdig and Achemenet; the tablets themselves from museums around the world. Fonts, map data, software and licences are on the credits & thanks page.

CDLI “no translation” means not recorded in CDLI — some works are published in RINAP, ETCSL, ORACC, etc. The catalog flags likely known projects.
