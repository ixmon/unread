Thank you

Unread Atlas stands on generations of work by excavators, museum curators, cataloguers, epigraphers and programmers, most of whom we will never meet. Every tablet on this site was dug up, conserved, numbered, photographed, copied or transliterated by someone else first. This page is our thank-you to them, and the place where we keep the licences straight.

Text corpora

The catalogue, find-spots, transliterations (ATF) and human English translations come from these projects. Transliterations are shown only where the source allows republication.

CDLI, the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative
The backbone of the catalogue: 426,657 records, find-spots, periods, museum numbers and ATF, from the cdli-gh bulk data. Reused with citation of CDLI, as CDLI asks. We do not copy CDLI images.
ORACC, the Open Richly Annotated Cuneiform Corpus, and its many projects
Lemmatised editions and human translations from RINAP, SAAo, ETCSRI, DCCLT, CAMS and dozens more. CC BY-SA 3.0. Some ORACC JSON files are marked CC0. Adaptations of ORACC material are shared under the same terms.
eBL, the electronic Babylonian Library (LMU Munich)
Catalogue metadata and the ATF-to-Unicode sign readings used by our cuneiform converter and font choice. Dataset MIT. eBL transliterations are searched for discovery only and are not republished here.
TLHdig, Thesaurus Linguarum Hethaeorum digitalis
Hittite and Anatolian texts. CC BY 4.0
Linguistically Annotated Achemenet Babylonian Texts (University of Helsinki), from Achemenet
Achaemenid-period Babylonian archives. CC BY 4.0
ETCSL, RINAP, SAA and the print series
We link to these and flag their tablets as already translated. Their publications are the scholarly record; cite them for published readings.

Fonts

Sign dictionary

The sign dictionary joins several sign lists and glossaries. Each English gloss on it is labelled with its source.

ePSD2, the electronic Pennsylvania Sumerian Dictionary (ORACC)
Most English glosses: guide words of Sumerian lemmas written with a single sign. CC0
ORACC: the Oracc Global Sign List (OGSL) and the SAAo, RINAP and RIBo Akkadian glossaries
Extra sign readings, Unicode mappings, and glosses for Akkadian logograms. CC0
electronic Babylonian Library (eBL), LMU Munich
The sign list (2,493 signs, readings, sign-list concordance numbers) and dictionary guide words for logograms. Used with attribution.
Wiktionary
Sumerian and Akkadian senses for a few signs no other source covers. CC BY-SA 4.0
CDLI proto-cuneiform sign list, Robert K. Englund
Drawings of archaic (Uruk-period) sign forms, via the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative. CC BY 4.0
Inferred glosses
A few glosses marked “inferred” are words that appear unusually often in CDLI's human English for lines containing the sign. They are a statistical hint, not a dictionary meaning.

Translation model

montycrypto/atf-to-english (B2c)
Every English line marked machine draft on the site is a cached draft from B2c, a fine-tune of Google's T5-large (Apache 2.0) trained on ATF and English pairs drawn from CDLI and ORACC translations (checkpoint ckpt-22000-hybrid-ct2bf16, also published for in-browser use as montycrypto/atf-to-english-onnx). Model MIT. A smaller Compact v4e (T5-small) build remains available in the on-device translator for phones and low-memory devices.

Privacy

We count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, a small script that sets no cookies, uses no local storage or fingerprinting, and collects no personal data: only page views, referring sites, country, browser type and page-load speed, in aggregate. Your IP address is not stored for analytics. We don't run ads or share data with anyone. Searches you type go to our own search API to return results; they are not tied to you. Our hosting (Amazon CloudFront) may keep standard server logs for security and capacity planning.
