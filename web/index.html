What's here

One searchable catalogue of 462,298 cuneiform tablets, merged from five open projects (CDLI, ORACC, eBL, TLHdig and Achemenet) with each tablet counted once. Every one has a page you can open, place on the map and search.

Why this exists

These are humanity's oldest known thoughts: receipts, prayers, letters, lullabies, and science. For generations, reading them took a PhD, a trip to a faraway land, and luck at the museum. The few glimpses most of us got came through some gatekeeper's filter. Unread Atlas faithfully organizes and presents this lost ancient writing, so we can all see what we can learn.

Many ways to look at the same clay

Every tablet is a point with a place, a date, a genre and a translation state. Each view lays those points out a different way, and your search comes with you between them.

Featured lead

What plant was tarmuš?

Translators render {ú}tar-muš as lupin in published translations, mostly in Neo-Assyrian medical recipes. Many medical tablets from Assur that name it have a transliteration but have never been read in English.

41. _{u2}kur-kur_ {u2}tar-musz {u2}_igi_-lim _{u2}igi-nisz_ {u2#}[x ...]
